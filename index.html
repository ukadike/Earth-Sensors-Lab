What this is

Earth Sensors Lab is a public-interest research lab, under Small Systems Lab, studying the relationship between environmental sensing, accessibility, and STEAM education. It asks how students, teachers, disabled learners, and communities can participate in scientific observation when sensors, data, AI, gardens, sound, touch, and local knowledge are treated as parts of one learning system.

This is not just about teaching STEM. It is about redesigning who gets to observe, measure, interpret, and explain the world — through low-cost tools, tactile learning, data sonification, and public-interest science education. Read the full research agenda.

Operations of Care

What is being sensed? Who owns the data? What should be shared publicly, and what should remain local to the school or community? How do students understand the sensor before trusting the data? How does the project work without perfect internet or expensive equipment?

Rule-Based Intelligence

ESL can use rule-based prompts and simple AI support for sensor anomaly flags, plain-language explanations, classroom observation logs, accessibility summaries, data quality warnings, and "do not overclaim" science checks.

Ancient Geometry

Circle: cycles of day and night, seasons, water, plant growth. Triangle: sensor, environment, human observer. Grid: garden plots, data tables, maps. Spiral: repeated observation and learning over time. Golden ratio: natural growth patterns and visual balance.

Curriculum modules

Seven modules, in development — observation first, devices second. No module has been field-tested in a real classroom yet.

  1. Observation Before Measurement
  2. What Is a Sensor?
  3. Soil, Water, Heat, and Air
  4. Data as Story / Sounding the Environment
  5. AI as Pattern Assistant
  6. Community Science / Build Your Own Observatory
  7. Observing Space

Accessibility baseline

Accessibility is not only compliance. It is a research method. If a chart cannot be heard, touched, described, translated, or discussed, the learning system is incomplete.

  • Tactile diagrams.
  • Audio descriptions.
  • Captions and transcripts.
  • Screen-reader-ready pages.
  • High contrast charts.
  • Low-cost hardware assumptions.
  • Offline classroom worksheets.

This baseline follows the same inclusive-design principles as Accessible by Design, the Small Systems Lab branch for accessibility research, audits, and tooling. Read more in ACCESSIBILITY.md.
