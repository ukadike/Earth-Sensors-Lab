Overview

The garden is the observatory

Earth Sensors Lab is a public-interest STEAM research initiative under Small Systems Lab. Mixed-ability student cohorts build instruments that observe soil, water, living systems, atmosphere, vibration, light, electromagnetic phenomena, and eventually the sky. Accessibility is part of the scientific architecture from the beginning.

Build to learn

QUESTION → DESIGN → BUILD → CALIBRATE → DEPLOY → OBSERVE → RECORD → INTERROGATE → REPRESENT → SHARE → REDESIGN.

Earth → Atmosphere → Space

One observatory can connect garden sensing, seismic and environmental observation, renewable energy, optical astronomy, radio observation, and multimodal interpretation.

Research

Measurement itself becomes an object of inquiry

Instrument literacy

What changes when students build, calibrate and repair an instrument rather than simply consume its readings?

Accessible scientific reasoning

How do visual, sonic, tactile, haptic and spoken representations affect pattern recognition, collaboration and interpretation?

Evidence + uncertainty

How can low-cost instrumentation teach calibration, quality assurance, provenance, limitations and responsible scientific claims?

Networked observatories

How can interoperable school/community observatories compare measurements across sites while preserving context and local agency?

Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the instrument

A visual graph is not treated as the scientific record with accessibility added afterward. Where appropriate, the same observation should support numerical/text, visual, sonic, speech, tactile and haptic representations.

Physical access

Reachable controls, wheelchair circulation, modular work surfaces, low-force/tactile controls and repair access.

Information access

Screen-readable structure, tactile/Braille identifiers, high contrast, sonification, audio description, captions and transcripts.

NASA / Space

A credible pathway from Earth observation to space science

Earth Sensors Lab does not claim NASA endorsement. The readiness work maps the observatory to authentic Earth science, heliophysics, astronomy, instrumentation, STEM-engagement and accessibility precedents while keeping research claims, partnerships and validation status explicit.
