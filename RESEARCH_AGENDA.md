# Research Agenda

Earth Sensors Lab investigates how learners understand the living
environment when data is made visible, audible, tactile, local, and
culturally meaningful.

This is a living agenda. Questions move between `OPEN QUESTION`, `EMERGING`
(early prototype or pilot evidence), and `PROBABLE` (supported by more than
one pilot or study) as work progresses — adapted from the status-tag
convention used across sibling Small Systems Lab repositories, applied here
to research claims rather than narrative canon.

See [/research/research-questions.md](research/research-questions.md) for the
full, versioned list with status tags.

## Research Thesis

Conventional STEM education often privileges visual charts, abstract
formulas, expensive instruments, and classroom-bound learning. Earth Sensors
Lab begins from a different premise: science learning can begin with direct
observation, community knowledge, environmental care, accessible tools, and
multisensory interpretation.

The lab studies how low-cost sensing systems and accessible data interfaces
can help learners build scientific confidence through place-based inquiry.

We are especially interested in how students learn when data can be heard,
touched, seen, discussed, translated, questioned, and connected to lived
environments.

This is not just about teaching STEM. It is about redesigning who gets to
observe, measure, interpret, and explain the world. Earth Sensors Lab is a
public-interest research lab studying the relationship between environmental
sensing, accessibility, and STEAM education. It asks how students, teachers,
disabled learners, and communities can participate in scientific observation
when sensors, data, AI, gardens, sound, touch, and local knowledge are
treated as parts of one learning system.

## Research Pillars

### 1. Accessible Observation

How do learners observe environmental change when visual perception is not
assumed as the default?

### 2. Multisensory Data

How can data become sound, texture, movement, speech, image, and story?

### 3. Community-Based Science

How can schools, gardens, and neighborhoods become research sites?

### 4. Low-Cost Instrumentation

How can inexpensive sensors expand access to scientific practice?

### 5. AI as Reflection Support

How can AI help learners notice patterns, ask better questions, and document
observations without replacing teachers or flattening local knowledge?

### 6. Teacher-Led Adaptation

How can teachers adapt sensing activities to local curricula, languages,
ability levels, and environments?

### 7. Climate and Ecological Literacy

How can students connect soil, heat, water, air, plants, and weather to
larger environmental systems?

Each pillar organizes one or more of the core research questions below, and
in turn organizes specific prototypes ([PROTOTYPES.md](PROTOTYPES.md)),
curriculum modules ([CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md)), and pilot designs
([PILOT_MODEL.md](PILOT_MODEL.md)). See
[/research/knowledge-contributions.md](research/knowledge-contributions.md)
for what the lab aims to actually contribute back from work on these
pillars, and
[/research/what-counts-as-data.md](research/what-counts-as-data.md) for how
broadly "data" is defined across all seven.

## Core research questions

- How can students learn environmental science through low-cost sensors?
- How can blind and low-vision learners participate in data observation?
- Can sound and touch make environmental data more understandable?
- How can AI support student reflection without replacing teacher judgment?
- How can community gardens become observatories?
- How can schools participate in research without carrying the financial
  burden of experimentation?
- How can local ecological knowledge become part of STEAM education?

Mapped to pillars: sensor-based learning and low-cost instrumentation →
Pillars 1 and 4; blind/low-vision participation and sound/touch
understanding → Pillars 1 and 2; AI support → Pillar 5; community gardens →
Pillar 3; schools carrying financial burden → partnership design, not a
pillar on its own (see [PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md](PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md)); local
ecological knowledge → Pillars 6 and 7.

## How the agenda is used

- Every prototype in [PROTOTYPES.md](PROTOTYPES.md) is tied to at least one
  research question it is trying to answer.
- Every curriculum module in [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md) records which
  question it investigates in its "research notes" section.
- Every pilot (see [PILOT_MODEL.md](PILOT_MODEL.md)) is expected to generate
  evidence — not conclusions — toward one or more of these questions.

## What this agenda is not

It is not a promise of results. No question on this page has a completed
answer yet. Where a pilot or prototype produces early evidence, that evidence
is recorded in [/research/findings-template.md](research/findings-template.md)
with its own status tag, never folded silently into the question itself.
