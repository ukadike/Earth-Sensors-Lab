# Accessibility Principles

The principles behind the baseline in [ACCESSIBILITY.md](../ACCESSIBILITY.md).

## 1. Accessibility is a research question

Earth Sensors Lab treats "how do we make this accessible" as something to
investigate, not something assumed solved by following a checklist. See
[RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md).

## 2. Design multisensory from the start

Activities and interfaces are designed with touch, sound, sight, and language
as co-equal channels from the first draft — not a visual design with
accommodations added afterward. See
[blind-low-vision-mode.md](blind-low-vision-mode.md) and
[tactile-learning.md](tactile-learning.md).

## 3. No meaning conveyed by a single channel alone

No color-only meaning, no hover-only meaning, no sound-only alert without a
visual or tactile equivalent, and vice versa.

## 4. Plain language is not "dumbed down"

Plain-language summaries exist alongside, not instead of, full documentation.
Complexity is preserved; jargon and unnecessarily complex sentence structure
are not.

## 5. Offline and low-bandwidth are accessibility issues too

A tool that only works with reliable high-speed internet excludes
participants as surely as one without alt text. See
[/software/offline-field-companion.md](../software/offline-field-companion.md).

## 6. Accommodations are co-designed, not assumed

Where possible, specific accommodations are developed with input from people
with the relevant lived experience (e.g., blind/low-vision advisors,
teachers of students with mobility disabilities), not designed in the
abstract. See the "accessibility advisor" partner type in
[PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md](../PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md).

## 7. Verify, don't assert

No claim of accessibility compliance is made for a prototype or tool that
hasn't actually been tested against that claim. See
[wcag-checklist.md](wcag-checklist.md) for the concrete, auditable standard
this maps to.
