# Ethics and Consent

Earth Sensors Lab works with students, schools, and communities. This
document sets the rules for how that work handles privacy, consent, and
data — before any pilot begins, not after.

## Student privacy

- Student data is minimized by default: only what a specific research
  question or classroom activity requires is collected.
- No student is identified by name in any public research output, dashboard,
  or published finding. Data is aggregated or anonymized before it leaves the
  classroom or site.

## School consent

- No school participates in a pilot, study, or data-collection activity
  without an explicit, written agreement with the institution (see
  [PILOT_MODEL.md](PILOT_MODEL.md)).
- The agreement states what is being observed, what is collected, who owns
  it, and how it can be withdrawn.

## Parent/guardian consent

- Where a pilot involves minors, parent or guardian consent is obtained
  before any student-specific data is collected — not assumed from school
  enrollment alone.
- Consent materials are written in plain language and offered in the
  languages spoken by the participating families wherever feasible.

## Photo/video consent

- Photos or video of students or identifiable community members require
  separate, explicit consent from what covers sensor or environmental data.
- Consent for photo/video is opt-in, not opt-out, and is revocable.

## Data minimization

- Environmental sensor data (soil, weather, light, sound) is the default
  focus — not behavioral or biometric data about people.
- Where audio sensing is used (see
  [/hardware/audio-sensing.md](hardware/audio-sensing.md)), it is scoped to
  environmental/biodiversity sound, not conversation or voice capture, unless
  a separate and explicit consent process says otherwise.

## Environmental data handling

- Site-level environmental data (a school garden's soil moisture, a
  community garden's microclimate) is understood as belonging to the site
  and its stewards first. Publication of aggregated or de-identified data
  requires the site partner's agreement.
- Raw sensor data stays local to the classroom or site unless a partner
  agreement specifies otherwise.

## No surveillance framing

- Earth Sensors Lab does not build or frame any tool as monitoring people.
  Sensors observe soil, air, water, light, and sound in an environment — not
  individuals' movement, attendance, or behavior.
- Any prototype proposal that could plausibly be read as surveillance is
  flagged as an `OPEN QUESTION` and does not proceed without explicit review.

## AI transparency

- Where AI-supported tools are used (see
  [/software/ai-reflection-assistant.md](software/ai-reflection-assistant.md)),
  their role, limits, and the fact that a student or teacher is interacting
  with an AI-assisted tool are disclosed plainly.
- AI tools support reflection and pattern-noticing; they do not grade, assess,
  or replace teacher judgment. See the "AI as Pattern Assistant" module in
  [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md).

## Accessibility accommodations

- Consent and data materials themselves must meet the baseline in
  [ACCESSIBILITY.md](ACCESSIBILITY.md) — plain language, screen-reader
  readable, available in non-digital/printable form.
- No participant is required to use an inaccessible consent process to take
  part in a pilot.

## Community ownership

- Community partners (gardens, libraries, museums) retain ownership of data
  collected at their site and decide what is shared publicly versus kept
  local — mirroring the Operations of Care questions in
  [SSL-METHOD.md](SSL-METHOD.md).
- Findings drawn from a partner site are shared back with that partner before
  or alongside any public release.
