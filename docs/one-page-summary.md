# One-Page Summary

## What is Earth Sensors Lab?

An open research laboratory, under Small Systems Lab, studying how
environmental sensing, creative technology, accessibility, AI, community
science, and multisensory learning can expand participation in STEAM
education. It builds prototypes, documents methods, and develops curriculum
— it is not a school program or paid enrichment vendor.

## Why does it matter?

Environmental sensing and data literacy are increasingly central to STEAM
education, but the tools and curricula around them rarely serve blind and
low-vision learners, low-bandwidth classrooms, or communities without lab
budgets. Earth Sensors Lab treats accessibility, low cost, and research
rigor as one design problem.

## What does it build?

Prototype low-cost environmental sensor kits; accessible data dashboards,
sonification tools, and tactile data cards; modular curriculum for
classrooms, gardens, museums, and libraries; and open research
documentation any lab could reuse. All current prototypes are documented in
[PROTOTYPES.md](../PROTOTYPES.md) — none is a finished, shipped product yet.

## Who can partner?

Schools, universities, museums, libraries, community gardens, accessibility
advisors, curriculum partners, hardware/software collaborators, and
funders/sponsors — as research collaborators and pilot sites, not customers.
See [PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md](../PARTNERSHIP_MODEL.md).

## What research questions guide it?

- How can students learn environmental science through low-cost sensors?
- How can blind and low-vision learners participate in data observation?
- Can sound and touch make environmental data more understandable?
- How can AI support student reflection without replacing teacher judgment?
- How can community gardens become observatories?
- How can schools participate in research without carrying the financial
  burden of experimentation?
- How can local ecological knowledge become part of STEAM education?

Full agenda: [RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md).

## What funding does it need?

Grants, fellowships, university partnerships, foundations, sponsors, public
agencies, or philanthropic support — to fund prototype development, pilot
partnerships, and accessibility research, per
[ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) Phase 4 (Funding Readiness). No specific funding
amount is stated here, since none has been finalized.
