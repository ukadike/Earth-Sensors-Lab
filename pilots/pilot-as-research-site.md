# Pilot as Research Site

A pilot is not proof that the model already works. A pilot is a structured
learning environment.

This distinction matters because it changes what counts as a successful
pilot. A pilot where a prototype broke, a curriculum module confused
students, or an accessibility accommodation failed is not a failed pilot —
it is a pilot that did exactly what a research site is for, provided the
failure is documented honestly rather than hidden.

## What each pilot should document

- what was tested
- who participated
- what accessibility needs appeared
- what students understood
- what teachers changed
- what tools failed
- what data was useful
- what ethical concerns emerged
- what should be redesigned

This list expands the evaluation framework in
[/research/pilot-evaluation.md](../research/pilot-evaluation.md) with a
specific emphasis on failure, adaptation, and redesign — not only on
outcomes that went as planned.

## Why "what tools failed" and "what should be redesigned" are required, not optional

A pilot report that only lists successes is not usable research — it gives
the next site no way to avoid the same problems. Recording tool failures and
needed redesigns is what makes a pilot a genuine contribution to
[/research/knowledge-contributions.md](../research/knowledge-contributions.md)
rather than a promotional summary.
