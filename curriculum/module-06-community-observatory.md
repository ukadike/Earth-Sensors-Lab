# Module 6: Community Science / Build Your Own Observatory

Status: proposed (framework drafted; depends on prior modules and a real
site partnership before it can run)

Covers two related themes: contributing to and using community-scale
observation (**Community Science**) and setting up an ongoing observation
site (**Build Your Own Observatory**).

## Learning goals

- Understand how individual classroom observations can combine into a
  larger, site-level or community-level picture.
- Learn what it takes to set up a sustained, low-maintenance observation
  point (a "mini observatory") at a school, garden, or community site.
- Practice thinking about data stewardship: who looks after readings over
  time, and who they belong to.

## Materials

- Cumulative data and skills from Modules 1–5.
- [Community Observatory Kit concept](../PROTOTYPES.md) (proposed; no kit
  built yet).
- A real or hypothetical ongoing site (garden, schoolyard corner, windowsill)
  for planning purposes.

## Accessibility notes

- Observatory planning should include accessibility of the physical site
  itself (path access, seating, tactile signage) alongside data
  accessibility — see [ACCESSIBILITY.md](../ACCESSIBILITY.md).
- Community-facing observatory materials (signage, instructions) should meet
  the same plain-language and multilingual-readiness baseline as classroom
  materials.

## Activity

1. As a class, review what's been learned across Modules 1–5 about
   observation, sensing, data, and reflection.
2. Discuss what would be needed to keep observing a chosen site on an
   ongoing basis (who checks it, how often, what's recorded).
3. Draft a simple observatory plan: what's measured, how often, who's
   responsible, and how data is shared or kept private, referencing
   [ETHICS_AND_CONSENT.md](../ETHICS_AND_CONSENT.md) community-ownership
   principles.
4. If pursuing a real ongoing observatory, this plan feeds into a pilot per
   [PILOT_MODEL.md](../PILOT_MODEL.md) and the relevant template in
   [/pilots/](../pilots/).

## Reflection questions

- What would make this observation site useful to people beyond your
  classroom?
- Who should decide what's shared publicly from this site's data?
- What would happen to this observatory if no one maintained it? What's the
  minimum care it needs to keep going?

## Research notes

Investigates: *How can community gardens become observatories?* and *How can
local ecological knowledge become part of STEAM education?* This module is
explicitly the bridge from classroom curriculum to a real pilot — see
[PILOT_MODEL.md](../PILOT_MODEL.md).

## Teacher adaptation notes

- This module is designed as a planning and discussion exercise even where
  no ongoing observatory will actually be built — the thinking is the
  learning goal, not necessarily a physical outcome.
- Where a school has an existing community garden partnership, this module
  can connect directly to
  [community-garden-pilot-template.md](../pilots/community-garden-pilot-template.md).
