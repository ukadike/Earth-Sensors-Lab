# Module 3: Soil, Water, Heat, and Air

Status: in development (draft curriculum, not yet field-tested)

## Learning goals

- Understand what soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and air conditions
  mean for plant and ecosystem health.
- Take and record real (or simulated, if no kit is available) readings for
  each of these four variables.
- Connect a single reading to a larger pattern over time (a preview of
  Module 4).

## Materials

- Soil moisture and temperature/humidity sensor components, if available
  (see [/hardware/soil-moisture.md](../hardware/soil-moisture.md) and
  [/hardware/temperature-humidity.md](../hardware/temperature-humidity.md)).
- Student workbook data-recording page.
- Where no hardware is available: printed sample data sets for discussion
  (clearly labeled as sample/practice data, not real site data).

## Accessibility notes

- Readings should be available in numeric, spoken/audio, and (where
  feasible) tactile form — see
  [sonification.md](../accessibility/sonification.md) and
  [tactile-learning.md](../accessibility/tactile-learning.md).
- Garden or outdoor activity components should have an indoor/tactile
  alternative for students with mobility constraints or environmental
  sensitivities.

## Activity

1. Select a small garden bed, planter, or outdoor site.
2. Take one reading each of soil moisture, temperature, and humidity (or
   review provided sample data if no kit is available).
3. Record readings in the workbook alongside a plain-language observation
   (e.g., "soil felt dry to the touch").
4. Discuss what a plant or organism at that site might need based on the
   readings.

## Reflection questions

- Did the sensor reading match what your senses told you? Where did they
  agree or disagree?
- What would you want to know before deciding whether a plant needs water?
- What single reading would be misleading without more context (e.g., time
  of day, recent rain)?

## Research notes

Investigates: *How can students learn environmental science through
low-cost sensors?* and *How can local ecological knowledge become part of
STEAM education?* — by pairing sensor readings with students' and
communities' existing knowledge of a site.

## Teacher adaptation notes

- Can be run with a single sensor type if a full kit isn't available.
- Encourage students to bring in local/family knowledge about the site's
  plants or soil — this module is designed to make room for that knowledge
  alongside sensor data, not replace it.
