# Module 4: Data as Story / Sounding the Environment

Status: in development (draft curriculum, not yet field-tested)

Covers two related themes: turning a series of readings into a story
(**Data as Story**) and listening to an environment as data
(**Sounding the Environment**).

## Learning goals

- Turn a series of individual readings (from Module 3) into a trend or story
  over time.
- Practice listening to environmental sound (wind, birds, insects, water) as
  a form of data, not just background.
- Compare a visual chart of a trend to a sonified or narrated version of the
  same trend.

## Materials

- Recorded readings from Module 3, or provided sample data sets.
- Audio sensing component if available (see
  [/hardware/audio-sensing.md](../hardware/audio-sensing.md)), or a quiet
  outdoor listening session as a low-tech substitute.
- [p5.js visualization concept](../software/p5-visualizations.md) and
  [sonification concept](../accessibility/sonification.md), where a working
  prototype exists to demonstrate.

## Accessibility notes

- This module is designed so the "story" of the data can be told through
  sound or narration as validly as through a visual chart — see
  [sonification.md](../accessibility/sonification.md).
- Sound-based activities should have a captioned/transcribed record for
  Deaf or hard-of-hearing students.

## Activity

1. Review the readings collected across Module 3 (or several days of a
   single reading type) and plot or narrate them as a trend.
2. Spend a quiet listening session recording (in words or audio) what can be
   heard in the environment at the same site.
3. If a sonification prototype is available, compare hearing the numeric
   trend as sound to hearing the environment's actual sound.
4. Discuss: what story does the data tell? What story does the environment's
   sound tell? Where do they agree or add new information?

## Reflection questions

- What pattern did you notice across multiple readings that a single
  reading wouldn't show?
- What did the environment's sound tell you that a number couldn't?
- If you had to explain this trend to someone who couldn't see a chart, how
  would you do it?

## Research notes

Investigates: *Can sound and touch make environmental data more
understandable?* — this module is the primary curricular test bed for that
question, directly comparing visual, sonic, and narrative representations of
the same data.

## Teacher adaptation notes

- The sound-listening activity works with no hardware at all — a quiet
  outdoor period is sufficient.
- For classrooms without any sensor kit, sample data sets (clearly labeled as
  such) can substitute for the data-as-story half of this module.
