# Earth Sensors Lab — Full Curriculum Guide

A single, sequenced guide to all six Earth Sensors Lab curriculum modules,
for a teacher, reviewer, or partner who wants the whole picture in one read.
The individual module files remain the source of truth for full detail —
this guide summarizes and sequences them. All content below is `in
development`: grade bands and time estimates are suggested starting points
for adaptation, not field-tested recommendations. See
[PILOT_MODEL.md](../PILOT_MODEL.md) for how real classroom testing would
change these numbers.

## How to use this guide

1. Read [Curriculum Philosophy](#curriculum-philosophy) once, before running
   any module — it explains why Module 1 has no sensor in it.
2. Use the [Sequence at a Glance](#sequence-at-a-glance) table to plan which
   modules fit your time, site, and equipment.
3. Run modules using the full detail in their own files; use this guide's
   per-module summaries as a quick refresher, not a replacement.
4. Use the [Field Notebook Model](field-notebook-model.md) throughout, in
   whatever format (paper, shared chart, audio journal) fits your site.
5. After running a module, feed what happened back through
   [PILOT_MODEL.md](../PILOT_MODEL.md) and
   [/research/findings-template.md](../research/findings-template.md) — this
   guide gets more accurate every time real classroom evidence comes in.

## Curriculum Philosophy

The curriculum begins with observation, not devices. Students first learn to
notice soil texture, plant growth, heat, shade, moisture, insects, sound,
weather, human activity, and change over time — before any sensor is
introduced. Sensors are extensions of observation, not replacements for
human attention. See [CURRICULUM.md](../CURRICULUM.md) for the full
argument and [field-notebook-model.md](field-notebook-model.md) for the tool
that carries it through every module.

## Module 1: Observation Before Measurement

**What it is:** Five minutes of unaided, multisensory observation of a
chosen site, recorded in plain descriptive language — no numbers yet.
**Why it's first:** Establishes noticing as a skill in its own right, so
later sensor readings are compared against a real baseline of attention
rather than introduced as the only valid way to "measure" a place.
**Full detail:** [module-01-observation.md](module-01-observation.md)

## Module 2: What Is a Sensor?

**What it is:** Comparing a human sense (touch, hearing) to its sensor
equivalent (thermistor, soil moisture probe), to build the vocabulary of
"signal," "translation," and "reading" used in every later module.
**Why it's second:** Introduces sensors as something to question, not
something to trust automatically — directly continuing the "do not
overclaim" habit from Module 1.
**Full detail:** [module-02-sensors.md](module-02-sensors.md)

## Module 3: Soil, Water, Heat, and Air

**What it is:** Taking real (or sample) soil moisture, temperature, and
humidity readings at a chosen site, paired with a plain-language sensory
observation of the same conditions.
**Why it's third:** First module where sensor data and human observation
are recorded side by side, so students can see directly where they agree
and where they don't.
**Full detail:** [module-03-soil-and-water.md](module-03-soil-and-water.md)

## Module 4: Data as Story / Sounding the Environment

**What it is:** Turning a series of readings into a trend, and listening to
an environment's actual sound as its own form of data — then comparing a
visual chart, a sonified trend, and a real listening session for the same
site.
**Why it's fourth:** The main test bed for whether sound and touch can make
environmental data more understandable, once students have real data (from
Module 3) to work with.
**Full detail:** [module-04-sound-and-data.md](module-04-sound-and-data.md)

## Module 5: AI as Pattern Assistant

**What it is:** Reviewing an AI-generated (or example) observation about a
data trend and critically evaluating what it got right, what it missed, and
whose judgment should be final.
**Why it's fifth:** Requires real data and a real trend (from Modules 3–4)
to give students something concrete to check the AI's output against.
**Full detail:** [module-05-ai-and-patterns.md](module-05-ai-and-patterns.md)
· Boundaries any such tool must respect:
[/software/ai-boundaries.md](../software/ai-boundaries.md)

## Module 6: Community Science / Build Your Own Observatory

**What it is:** Planning an ongoing observation site — what's measured, how
often, by whom, and what's shared publicly versus kept local.
**Why it's last:** Synthesizes everything from Modules 1–5 into a plan for
sustained, real-world observation, and is the direct bridge into an actual
pilot (see [PILOT_MODEL.md](../PILOT_MODEL.md)).
**Full detail:**
[module-06-community-observatory.md](module-06-community-observatory.md)

## What this guide does not claim

No module in this sequence has been run start-to-finish in a real classroom
or community site as of this writing. Grade bands, timing, and the "best
with kit" / "optional" equipment notes above are design judgments meant to
help a first pilot get started — not validated outcomes. The first real
pilot to run this sequence should treat every number on this page as a
hypothesis to test, and its results should be fed back through
[/research/findings-template.md](../research/findings-template.md), which
will let this guide be revised with real evidence rather than best-guess
defaults.
