# What Counts as Data?

Earth Sensors Lab treats data broadly.

Data may include:

- sensor readings
- student observations
- field notes
- drawings
- audio recordings
- tactile maps
- soil descriptions
- plant growth logs
- weather notes
- community memory
- teacher reflections
- accessibility barriers
- student questions
- failed experiments
- local ecological knowledge

This matters because accessible STEAM education cannot depend only on
numerical readings or visual dashboards. A learning system that only counts
sensor output as "real data" quietly excludes the blind student who noticed
a change in soil smell before any moisture sensor registered it, the teacher
who observed that an activity confused half the class, and the grandparent
who knows when a particular tree usually flowers.

## Why this is a research stance, not just a curriculum note

Deciding what counts as data is a research design choice with consequences.
If Earth Sensors Lab defined data narrowly — numeric sensor readings only —
it would structurally exclude several of its own
[research pillars](../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md#research-pillars): Accessible
Observation and Multisensory Data cannot be studied using a definition of
data that assumes sight and instrumentation as prerequisites.

## How this connects to the rest of the repository

- **Curriculum:** the
  [field notebook model](../curriculum/field-notebook-model.md) is built to
  capture exactly this range of data types, not just sensor values.
- **Software:** the
  [reasoning layers](../software/reasoning-layers.md) treat "Observation"
  (Layer 1) as distinct from and prior to "Measurement" (Layer 2) — both are
  data, neither is optional.
- **Pilots:** [pilot-as-research-site.md](../pilots/pilot-as-research-site.md)
  asks pilots to document "what data was useful," which explicitly includes
  non-numeric forms.
- **Findings:** [findings-template.md](findings-template.md) should draw on
  this full range of data, not only sensor logs, when recording evidence.

## What this does not mean

Treating data broadly does not mean treating all data as equally certain or
equally generalizable. A student's field note is evidence of what that
student noticed; it is not a substitute for a calibrated sensor reading when
a calibrated reading is what a research question actually requires. Breadth
of what counts as data is not license to blur what a specific claim is
actually supported by — see the "do not overclaim" principle in
[SSL-METHOD.md](../SSL-METHOD.md).
