# Earth Sensors Lab — Instrument Standard

Every Earth Sensors Lab instrument is a student-built research and learning system. This standard applies to soil, atmosphere, vibration, light, electromagnetic, astronomical, energy, water, biodiversity, and future instrument families.

## Required Package

Each instrument must include:

1. Overview
2. Schema Card
3. Research questions
4. Learning objectives
5. Scientific principles
6. Instrument context and precedents
7. Parts and materials
8. Safety requirements
9. Build instructions
10. Electronic/mechanical schematic
11. Figure or technical drawing
12. Accessible figure description
13. ASCII/text representation
14. Code, when applicable
15. Calibration procedure
16. Field/experiment protocol
17. Data schema and observation records
18. Analysis and uncertainty questions
19. Multisensory representations
20. Student research notebook prompts
21. Installation/public demonstration
22. Workshop activity
23. Troubleshooting
24. Reflection and redesign

## Research Cycle

**QUESTION → DESIGN → BUILD → CALIBRATE → DEPLOY → OBSERVE → RECORD → INTERROGATE → REPRESENT → SHARE → REDESIGN**

Students must understand what the instrument measures, how the transducer produces a signal, how that signal becomes data, what calibration establishes, what uncertainty remains, and what conclusions the evidence does and does not support.

## Accessibility Rule

Accessibility is part of the instrument specification from the beginning. Where appropriate, the same canonical observation must support parallel representations such as numerical/text, visual, sonic, spoken, tactile, and haptic forms.

No representation is automatically treated as the primary or 'real' version solely because it is visual.

## Visual Documentation Rule

Every significant schematic or figure must have:

- a human-readable caption;
- an accessible description explaining relevant relationships and spatial organization;
- a text/ASCII representation when the relationship can be meaningfully expressed in text;
- stable identifiers connecting the figure to its instrument and Schema Card.

Technical accuracy takes precedence over decorative rendering.

## Small Systems Lab Rule

Every instrument and its associated curriculum, observations, experiments, representations, and findings must be addressable through stable identifiers and structured records.

Every instrument must carry a Schema Card sufficient for Omoluabi to relate it codifiably to other Earth Sensors Lab instruments, curricula, observations, accessibility representations, and relevant Small Systems Lab projects.

## Evidence and Status

Use explicit status labels:

- `proposed` — specified but not yet built;
- `prototype` — physically or computationally built;
- `tested` — tested under a documented protocol;
- `validated` — meets a defined validation standard with evidence.

Do not fabricate testing, accessibility validation, scientific findings, partnerships, deployments, or student outcomes.

## Omoluabi Interoperability

Omoluabi must be able to trace, where records exist:

**observation → instrument → sensor/transducer → calibration → protocol → applicable rule → related observations → evidence → uncertainty → curriculum → accessible representations**

Omoluabi supports interrogation of the evidence. It does not replace measurement, scientific judgment, student reasoning, or human review.

## Cross-System Relationships

Schema Cards may explicitly identify relationships to:

- **Omoluabi** — rules, evidence, provenance, contradiction, uncertainty, and interrogation;
- **Ìjóyà** — touch, vibration, embodiment, interdependence, soft systems, and environmental response;
- **UMADA** — speculative counterparts and future extensions of present-day research systems.

These relationships must be explicit fields, not inferred solely from prose.
